# Project Overview

Sharnay Photography · Brand System
Document 01 · Revision A — July 2026

---

## Project Vision

Sharnay Photography should be recognizable before the logo is visible.

That is the whole ambition, stated plainly. A viewer scrolling past a frame, a client opening a print sleeve, a stranger seeing a card on a table — each should register a particular quality of attention before they read a name. Proportion, spacing, restraint, and the confidence to leave things out.

This is achievable because photography already has a visual language of its own. Apertures, frames, focal planes, contact sheets, registration marks, f-stops, the geometry of a lens barrel. These forms are precise, beautiful, and understood without explanation. They are also, crucially, not decorative — every one of them exists because it does something.

We are not inventing a look for Sharnay Photography. We are drawing the identity out of the mechanics of the craft itself.

---

## Project Goals

**A language, not a logo.** The mark is one output of the system, not the system. Success is measured by how well the identity holds in the places the logo never appears.

**One geometry behind everything.** Marks, icons, crops, grids, and transitions should share a construction. Anything drawn a year from now should look inevitable next to what we draw this month.

**Layouts that behave like printed pages.** Margins with intent, a real typographic hierarchy, captions that mean something. Editorial discipline applied to screens.

**A website that is a gallery, not a portfolio template.** The images set the pace. The interface gets out of the way and stays out.

**A social system that is fast to use.** Templates that take five minutes, not fifty, and that still look composed at the end of a long week. A system nobody uses is a system that failed.

**Print that confirms the promise.** The physical pieces are where a premium claim is either believed or quietly disbelieved. Paper, weight, and finish do work that pixels cannot.

**Documentation precise enough to hand over.** Another designer, another studio, or Sharnay alone should be able to extend this system correctly without us in the room.

## Design Philosophy

Three convictions shape every decision in this project.

### The brand frames the work

Photographs are the content. The identity is the frame, the mat, and the wall. A frame that draws attention to itself has failed at the only job it has. Every element we add must earn its place by improving how the photography reads — not by making the brand more visible.

### Restraint is the premium signal

Expensive design is not busy. It is precise. The perception of quality comes from resolved proportions, consistent spacing, disciplined type, and generous negative space — not from more. Where we are tempted to add, we sharpen instead.

### Systems age better than styles

A style is a moment. A system is a set of relationships, and relationships survive changing taste. By deriving the identity from photographic geometry rather than from current design fashion, we build something that will still be correct when the fashion turns over, which it will.

The full articulation of these positions is in [`design-principles.md`](design-principles.md).

## Expected Deliverables

### Written system
Project overview, roadmap, visual language system, photographer's vocabulary, design principles, implementation plan. Complete brand guidelines as a designed, distributable document.

### Identity
Primary mark, monogram, aperture construction drawings, lockups, clear-space and minimum-size rules, incorrect-use documentation.

### Visual language
Typographic system with scale and specimens. Colour system with application ratios. Icon family on a single grid. Editorial elements — rules, frame numbers, index marks, credit lines. Layout grids derived from photographic aspect ratios. Motion principles with timing and easing.

### Website
Design and build of the public site: work, series, about, services, journal, contact. Gallery behaviour, image delivery standards, responsive layouts, motion implementation.

### Social system
Instagram grid strategy, post and carousel templates, story templates, caption conventions, a working method for weekly use.

### Print system
Business card, print sleeve or folio, thank-you card, seal or sticker, pricing and service guide, packaging direction. Paper, ink, and finish specifications ready for a printer.

### Assets
Master logo files in every required format, exported icon sets, mockups for presentation, an organized photography library, and a clean export pipeline.

## Current Phase

**Phase 1 · Foundation**

Establishing the repository architecture and the written system that governs everything after it. This phase produces no visual work by design. Its purpose is to make the visual work that follows fast, coherent, and defensible.

Phase 1 is complete when the six core documents are written, the folder architecture is in place with its conventions, and the direction has been reviewed and agreed with the client.

## Open Questions

Held here deliberately. These are answered with the client, not assumed by the studio, and each one is resolved before the phase that depends on it.

1. **Practice focus.** Which work leads: portrait, editorial, wedding, commercial, documentary? The hierarchy shapes tone more than any other single input.
2. **Client profile.** Who is being spoken to, and what do they already believe about photographers before they arrive?
3. **Name usage.** Is the brand *Sharnay*, *Sharnay Photography*, or both depending on surface?
4. **Voice.** Does Sharnay write in first person, or does the studio speak in third?
5. **Colour tolerance.** How far from near-monochrome is the client willing to go? The recommendation is: not far.
6. **Print budget.** Which finishes are realistically available — this determines whether the print system is designed around ink, embossing, or paper alone.
7. **Existing equity.** Is there any current mark, colour, or typeface with recognition worth carrying forward?
