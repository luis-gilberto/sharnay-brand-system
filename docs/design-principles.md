# Design Principles

Sharnay Photography · Brand System
Document 05 · Revision A — July 2026

---

Ten principles. They exist to settle arguments, including the ones we will have with ourselves at two in the morning near the end of a phase.

Each carries a test — a question with an answer, not a sentiment. A principle that cannot be applied to a specific decision is a slogan, and slogans have no authority.

---

### 01 · Photography first

The photographs are the product. Everything else is packaging, and packaging that upstages the product is a mistake regardless of how well it is made.

This has consequences we should accept now: some of our best ideas will not survive, because they will be too good. An element that makes people notice the design in a piece where the photography is strong has failed at its job, however elegant it is.

**The test** — Remove the design element. Is the photograph better? Then leave it removed.

---

### 02 · The brand frames the work. It never competes with it.

A frame's whole purpose is to define an edge and then stop. It is not the subject, and it does not want to be.

Practically: the identity sits at the margins, at the edges, in the space between images. It appears at the beginning and the end. It does not sit on top of a photograph unless there is a functional reason, and "it looked good" is not a functional reason.

**The test** — Where does the eye land first? If it is anything other than the image, redesign.

---

### 03 · Restraint over decoration

Every element in this system must be doing work. Rules organize. Numbers identify. Icons clarify. Space separates. Nothing exists to fill an area or to make a page feel more designed.

The instinct to add is almost always the wrong instinct. When a layout feels weak, the problem is usually proportion, spacing, or hierarchy — and none of those are solved by additions.

**The test** — What does this element do? If the answer contains the word "feel", it is decoration.

---

### 04 · Timeless over trendy

We are designing something that must look correct in a decade. That rules out most of what currently reads as contemporary — the gradient, the blur, the oversized display face of the moment, the interaction pattern everyone is copying this year.

The safeguard is derivation. Because the system comes from photographic geometry rather than from design fashion, it is anchored to something that has not changed in a century and will not change soon.

**The test** — Will this look like 2026 in 2036? If yes, and that is not the point, cut it.

---

### 05 · Every graphic element has a purpose

Ornament is not permitted. Not because ornament is inherently wrong, but because this identity's entire argument is that the work speaks for itself, and ornament contradicts that argument in every piece it appears in.

Each element must be traceable to one of the six concepts in the [photographer's vocabulary](photographer-vocabulary.md). If it cannot be traced, it is not part of the language.

**The test** — Which concept does it serve — light, frame, focus, perspective, depth, or print? Name it, or lose the element.

---

### 06 · Negative space is part of the design

Space is not what is left over after the content is placed. It is composed, specified, and defended.

This is also the principle most often broken under pressure, because there is always one more thing someone wants on the page. The answer is a second page. Margins are not a budget to be spent.

**The test** — Could this breathe more? Almost always yes. Give it the space before you give it anything else.

---

### 07 · Editorial before commercial

We are designing a publication that happens to sell something, not a sales instrument with pictures in it.

Editorial thinking means sequence, pacing, captions that inform, hierarchy that respects the reader, and the assumption that the audience is intelligent. Commercial thinking means urgency, repetition, and pressure. The first sells better at this level anyway, because the audience for premium photography is repelled by being sold to.

**The test** — Would this belong in a well-made magazine, or on a landing page? Choose the magazine.

---

### 08 · Premium through precision

Nothing in this system is expensive because it is elaborate. It is premium because it is exact.

Precision is measurable and unglamorous: consistent spacing, resolved optical alignment, correct typographic detailing, matched stroke weights, considered line breaks. It is the accumulation of small correctness, and its absence is felt even by people who cannot name it.

**The test** — Is every value in this piece drawn from the system, or did something get nudged until it looked right? Nudges are debt.

---

### 09 · Every icon belongs to the same family

One grid, one stroke, one construction, one set of angles. An icon that needs an exception is an icon that has not been solved yet.

The pressure against this principle arrives later, when a new service needs an icon quickly. The system's defence is that drawing a correct icon should be easier than drawing an incorrect one, which is a burden on the construction rules rather than on the person in a hurry.

**The test** — Place it in the set at a single size. Does anything stand out? Redraw the one that does.

---

### 10 · Motion should feel optical

Movement in this system comes from lenses and light, not from software convention. Things resolve, expose, and open. They do not slide, bounce, pop, or spring.

Motion also has a low ceiling: it clarifies sequence and relationship, and then it stops. Any animation that is enjoyable to watch a second time is already too much.

**The test** — Does it behave like something inside a camera, or like something inside a browser? Only the first is ours.

---

## When Principles Conflict

They will. The order above is the order of authority.

**Photography first** outranks everything, including our preferences and, occasionally, the client's. If a decision improves the design and diminishes the work, the work wins.

Where two principles genuinely tie, the tiebreaker is subtraction. Given two defensible options, take the quieter one. This system has never yet been made worse by removing something, and it will not be.
