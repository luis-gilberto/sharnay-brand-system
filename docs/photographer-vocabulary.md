# The Photographer's Vocabulary

Sharnay Photography · Brand System
Document 04 · Revision A — July 2026

---

Photographers already have a language. It is spoken in the field, in the edit, and in the darkroom, and it is unusually precise — every term in it describes a decision, and every decision has a visible consequence.

We are building the identity out of that language rather than around it.

Six ideas carry the whole system. They are not themes or moods; they are working concepts with real mechanics behind them, which is why they can generate design decisions instead of merely describing them. Every choice made in this project should be traceable to at least one.

**Light · Frame · Focus · Perspective · Depth · Print**

---

## I · Light

> *Everything is light. There is no photograph without it, and no two photographers use it the same way.*

### To a photographer

Light is the material. It has direction, quality, temperature, and intensity, and controlling it is most of the craft. The distinguishing skill is not adding light but shaping and subtracting it — knowing which part of the frame to let fall away. A photographer's relationship with light is the closest thing they have to a signature.

### In the identity

Light gives us the contrast structure of the whole system. The palette is built as exposure rather than as colour: paper, ink, and the range between them. Grey is not a neutral filler here — it is the mid-tone, and it does the same work in a layout that it does in a print.

It also sets the rule for emphasis. Highlight is precious. A single bright element on a restrained page reads as a highlight; three of them read as an overexposure. Colour, when it is used at all, is spent the way a photographer spends the brightest part of a frame.

### In layouts

Negative space is where the light falls. It is not leftover area — it is the exposure of the page, and it is specified with the same care as the content. Pages are composed with a deliberate tonal balance: how much of this spread is paper, how much is image, how much is ink.

Contrast between sections gives a document rhythm the way a sequence of light and shadow gives an edit rhythm.

### In iconography

Icons are drawn as light lines on ground, never as heavy shapes. The stroke is consistent so that a set of icons reads as evenly exposed. Any icon that appears darker or heavier than its siblings is a metering error, and is corrected optically rather than mathematically.

### On the website

Light is a design decision, not a toggle. The site has a defined tonal environment, and images are given surroundings that flatter rather than compete with them. Elements appear through exposure — resolving into presence rather than sliding into place. Interface elements sit in the mid-tones and step forward only when they are needed.

## II · Frame

> *A photograph is not what is in front of the camera. It is what the photographer decided to include.*

### To a photographer

Framing is authorship. The edges of the frame are the strongest decision available, because everything outside them is a deliberate exclusion. Photographers speak about the frame constantly: what it holds, where it cuts, how close the edge sits to the subject. Composition is the arrangement of that decision.

### In the identity

Frame is the reason the brand is a container rather than a subject. The identity's role is to establish edges and let the work sit inside them, and this is the single idea that most protects the photography from the design.

It also gives us our most useful graphic device: the line. A rule, a keyline, a corner mark, an unclosed frame. These are the least decorative marks available, and they are already photographic.

### In layouts

Layout becomes the placement of edges. Where the image stops and the page begins is a compositional choice, and the system gives it three distinct answers: full bleed, where the frame is the screen or the sheet; framed, where the image sits inside a defined margin; and contact sheet, where many frames are shown as a set.

Crops are treated as decisions with reasons, and images are never cropped merely to fill a slot in a grid.

### In iconography

Every icon is drawn inside the same keyline box, and that box is a frame. The consistency of what is included in it — the live area, the padding, the optical centring — is what makes the family read as a family.

Several icons will be frames themselves: format marks, crop indicators, aspect ratios. These are among the most useful forms in the set precisely because they carry no metaphor.

### On the website

The viewport is the frame, and the site treats it as one. Images are given edges deliberately: some meet the screen, some are held inside a margin. Galleries are sequences of frames, paced rather than dumped. The interface stays at the edge of the frame — never over the picture.

## III · Focus

> *Focus is a choice about what matters. Everything sharp is an argument.*

### To a photographer

A lens can only resolve one plane at a time. Choosing that plane is choosing the subject, and everything the photographer decides to leave soft is part of the same decision. Focus is not about clarity as a technical goal; it is about directing attention.

### In the identity

Focus is the argument for restraint, stated as a mechanic rather than as a preference. If everything on a page is sharp, nothing is the subject. The system therefore keeps most elements deliberately quiet so that the one thing that matters can be resolved.

It also sets the standard for craft. Where the system does resolve — the mark, the icon set, the type detailing — it must be genuinely sharp. Precision earns the right to be quiet everywhere else.

### In layouts

Every layout has a single subject, chosen before anything is placed. Hierarchy is built by softening rather than by shouting: secondary content is set smaller, lighter, and further from the eye rather than being pushed down by louder headings.

A page with two subjects is a page with none, and the fix is a second page.

### In iconography

Icons are drawn with a fixed level of detail — enough to be understood instantly, and nothing beyond that. Detail is removed until removing more would break recognition, then stopped. This keeps the family sharp at small sizes and prevents individual icons from becoming illustrations.

### On the website

Attention is directed rather than assumed. One thing is primary in every view. Motion is used to resolve elements rather than to move them, and focus states in the interface are treated literally — a focused element is the one the design has chosen to make sharp, which makes keyboard navigation an aesthetic opportunity rather than a compliance chore.

## IV · Perspective

> *Where the photographer stands is the whole photograph.*

### To a photographer

Perspective is position: the height of the camera, the distance to the subject, the focal length, the angle of approach. It cannot be fixed afterward. Two photographers in the same room with the same equipment produce different work almost entirely because they stand in different places.

### In the identity

Perspective is the argument for a distinct point of view over a competent one. The identity should be recognizably *from somewhere* rather than pleasantly generic, and that comes from taking positions: a specific angle set, a specific proportional system, a specific restraint.

Practically, perspective also governs the angle vocabulary. All diagonals in the system come from the aperture's rotational increment. Arbitrary angles are a change of viewpoint without a reason.

### In layouts

Distance is composed. Some spreads sit close — a tight crop, a large image, a short line length. Others step back — small images, generous margins, an index or contact-sheet view. Alternating that distance gives a document pace, in the same way that alternating wide and close frames gives a photo essay pace.

Layouts are also allowed asymmetry. A centred page has no viewpoint.

### In iconography

Icons are drawn from a consistent viewpoint: flat, frontal, orthographic. No perspective drawing, no dimensional tricks, no isometric exceptions. This is a rule with real consequences — a camera icon is drawn as a diagram of a camera, not as a picture of one.

Where an object cannot be represented frontally with clarity, it is represented by its function instead.

### On the website

Scroll is the viewer's movement through the work, and the site controls the distance at each step: a full-bleed frame is standing close; a grid is standing back. Series are sequenced to alternate that distance rather than presenting everything at the same scale.

Navigation is honest about position — the visitor always knows where they are standing in a body of work.

## V · Depth

> *A photograph is two dimensions arranged to describe three.*

### To a photographer

Depth is constructed: foreground, middle ground, background, separated by focus, tone, and scale. It is what makes a flat image feel inhabited. A photographer builds depth deliberately, often by finding something to put in the near field.

### In the identity

Depth gives the system layers without ornament. There is a foreground — the photography and the primary message. There is a middle ground — type, structure, and navigation. There is a background — the page itself, the tone, the space. Every element knows which layer it belongs to, and elements do not change layers to attract attention.

This is also how the system creates richness without adding elements: separation by tone and scale rather than by shadow and effect.

### In layouts

Layers are expressed through scale and weight rather than through overlap and shadow. A large image and a small caption occupy different planes because of their size relationship, not because one is on top of the other.

Where elements do overlap, it is structural and deliberate — an image bleeding past a column, a rule running under a heading — and it always resolves against the grid.

### In iconography

Depth is expressed only through occlusion: a form in front of another form, drawn as an intentional break in the line behind it. No drop shadows, no fills implying volume, no gradients. This keeps the family flat while still letting it describe objects that have parts.

The occlusion gap is specified once and used everywhere.

### On the website

Layered elements move at different rates when it clarifies their relationship, read as depth of field rather than as a parallax effect. Overlays sit at a defined elevation with a consistent treatment. Nothing floats without a reason, and nothing casts a shadow to look expensive.

## VI · Print

> *The photograph is finished when it exists as an object.*

### To a photographer

Print is the destination. An image on a screen is a proposal; a print is a commitment to size, paper, tone, and permanence. It is also where a photographer's standards are most exposed, because paper reveals everything a screen forgives.

### In the identity

Print is the discipline standard for the whole system. Every element is designed as though it will be printed: no reliance on screen colour, no forms that depend on backlight, no weights that will fill in on uncoated stock.

It is also the reason the system is built on real materials rather than effects. If it cannot be made physically, it is not part of the language.

### In layouts

Layouts are built with print logic even when they are destined for a screen: consistent margins, a real baseline relationship, captions in fixed positions, a considered relationship between page edge and content. This is the source of the editorial quality — pages designed to be printed simply behave better than pages designed to scroll.

Sequence matters too. A body of work has an order, an opening, and an ending, on paper and on screen alike.

### In iconography

Icons are specified for physical production, which means minimum sizes, minimum stroke widths at scale, single-colour reproduction, and legibility when foiled, embossed, or engraved. An icon that only works at 24px in a browser is a half-finished icon.

Every form is tested at the smallest size it will ever be produced at, before it is approved.

### On the website

The site inherits print's care: measured type, real hierarchy, captions that carry information, generous margins, and pages composed rather than assembled. Images are prepared with print-level attention to colour profile, tone, and compression.

The site should also make the physical work visible. Prints, sleeves, and delivered objects are photographed and shown, because the destination of the work is part of the offer.

---

## Using the Vocabulary

These six ideas are a working tool, not a manifesto. They are most useful as a test.

When a decision is unclear, ask which concept it serves. A graphic element that answers none of them is decoration, and decoration is removed. An element that answers three is probably a keystone of the system and should be developed further.

When a decision is contested, the concepts arbitrate. *Focus* will usually argue for removing something. *Frame* will usually argue for giving the photography more room. *Print* will usually argue for making it simpler so it can be produced. In this system, those instincts are correct more often than not.
