# Discovery

Sharnay Photography · Brand System
Working document · Phase 1 · Revision A — July 2026

---

## Before you begin

These questions exist because guessing is expensive.

Almost every decision we make over the next few months — the shape of the mark, how much colour the brand allows, whether the site speaks in your voice or ours — traces back to something on this page. Answering them now takes an hour. Discovering the answers later, after something has been designed around the wrong assumption, takes considerably longer.

A few things worth saying up front:

**Write the way you talk.** These answers become the raw material for how the brand sounds. Polished paragraphs are less useful to us than honest ones.

**"I don't know" is a real answer.** Several of these are questions you may never have had a reason to think about. Saying so tells us it's ours to propose rather than yours to decide, which is useful information.

**Short is fine.** A sentence is enough almost everywhere. Two of these deserve a paragraph, and they're marked.

---

## I · The Work

**1 · What kind of photography leads?**
If someone found your work through one image, which kind of image would you want it to be — portrait, editorial, wedding, commercial, documentary, something else?

*Why we ask: this shapes tone more than any other single answer. A documentary practice and a wedding practice want different levels of formality from everything, down to the weight of a line.*

**2 · What kind of work do you want more of?**
Not what pays now. What you'd take if the calendar were yours to fill.

*Why we ask: we design for where the practice is going, not where it has been.*

**3 · What do you do that another good photographer in your city doesn't?**
Take a paragraph on this one if you have it in you.

*Why we ask: this is the closest thing to the brief. Everything premium about an identity comes from having a genuine position, and positions come from real differences.*

**4 · Is there a photograph of yours you'd be happy to be judged on for the next five years?**
Send it. It doesn't have to be the most popular one.

---

## II · The People You Work For

**5 · Who is actually hiring you?**
Age, work, taste, budget — whatever you know. Describe a real client if that's easier than describing a type.

**6 · What do they believe about photographers before they meet you?**
Expectations, fears, past disappointments.

*Why we ask: an identity's job is partly to correct an assumption. Knowing which one lets us aim.*

**7 · Where do people find you now?**
Instagram, referral, search, a venue, an agency — roughly in order.

*Why we ask: this decides how much weight the social system carries relative to the website, and therefore which one we make harder-working.*

**8 · What makes someone choose you over someone cheaper?**
Their words if you've heard them say it.

---

## III · The Name and the Voice

**9 · Is the brand Sharnay, or Sharnay Photography?**
Or both, depending on where it appears? Say what feels right, even if you can't justify it.

**10 · How is it said aloud?**
Include pronunciation if it's ever been mistaken.

**11 · Do you write as yourself, or does the studio speak?**
"I photographed this in Marrakech" versus "Sharnay Photography documented this in Marrakech." One is not better than the other, but the brand can only pick one.

**12 · Three words you'd want a client to use about the experience of working with you.**
Not about the pictures. About you.

**13 · Anything you'd never want the brand to sound like?**
Sometimes easier to answer than the version above, and just as useful.

---

## IV · Colour and Feel

**14 · How much colour does this brand get?**
Our recommendation is very little — near-black, paper, and one restrained accent, letting your photographs carry all the colour in the system. Tell us if that sounds right, or if it sounds too austere for how you see the practice.

*Why we ask: this is the one recommendation we'd defend hardest, so we'd rather have the disagreement now than at the presentation.*

**15 · Is your work warm or cool?**
Look at thirty of your images together before answering. Most photographers are surprised.

**16 · Three brands — any industry — that feel right to you.**
And, more usefully: one line each on *why*. "Because it's clean" tells us nothing. "Because they trust you to work it out yourself" tells us a great deal.

**17 · Anything in photography branding you're tired of seeing?**
Be specific and be unkind. This is one of the more valuable answers on the page.

---

## V · The Physical

**18 · What do clients actually receive from you?**
Files, prints, an album, a USB, a box, an email link. Describe the current moment of delivery honestly, including the parts that feel unfinished.

**19 · What could that moment be?**
If it were the best part of working with you.

**20 · What's a realistic budget for printed pieces?**
A range is fine. This decides whether the print system is designed around good paper alone, or around foil, embossing, and specialist finishes.

*Why we ask: we'd rather design something modest and beautiful that you'll reorder for years than something expensive you'll print once.*

---

## VI · What Already Exists

**21 · Is there a current logo, colour, or typeface with any recognition worth keeping?**
Even if you dislike it. If clients recognize it, that's an asset we should consider before discarding.

**22 · What's currently live?**
Site, Instagram, printed pieces, anything with your name on it. Links or photographs.

**23 · Is anything locked?**
A domain you've paid for, a name registered, a piece already printed in quantity, a partnership with its own requirements.

---

## Three things to send with your answers

**A selection of your work.** Thirty to fifty images that represent where the practice is going. Not your greatest hits — a fair sample, including the everyday jobs. Every grid, layout, and template in this project gets tested against these, and placeholder images hide problems that real photographs reveal immediately.

**Anything printed.** Photographs of current cards, packaging, or delivered work, however rough.

**Anything you love.** A book, a magazine, a shop interior, a bottle, a website. No explanation needed if you can't find one.

---

## What happens next

We read all of it, then come back with the direction stated in three sentences.

Not a mood board and not a logo — three sentences describing what this identity is and what it refuses to be. If you read them and recognize your practice, we start drawing. If you don't, we're one conversation from getting it right instead of three months and a presentation.

That's the whole gate. Everything after it moves quickly.
