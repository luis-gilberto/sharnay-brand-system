# Visual Language System

Sharnay Photography · Brand System
Document 03 · Revision A — July 2026

---

**This document describes intent, not execution.** Nothing here is designed yet. Each system is defined by what it must accomplish, what constrains it, and what remains open — so that when we begin drawing in Phase 2, we are solving a stated problem rather than searching for one.

---

## 01 · Purpose

A visual language is what lets a brand produce work it has never seen before and have it come out right.

Sharnay Photography will need hundreds of individual pieces over the next few years: a rate card, a story template, a print stamp, an email signature, an icon for a service that does not exist yet. Designing each of those from scratch produces drift. Designing the rules that generate them produces coherence.

This system's purpose is to hold that coherence across three conditions:

**Across media.** A form drawn for a screen must survive being foil-stamped at 12mm on a card.

**Across time.** Work made in year three must sit beside work made in month one without either looking dated.

**Across hands.** Sharnay, a printer, a developer, or another studio must all be able to produce correct work from these rules.

---

## 02 · Core Philosophy

### Generated, not assembled

Most identities are collections: a typeface chosen for feel, an icon set chosen for convenience, a colour chosen from a mood board. Collections are held together by taste alone, and taste is not transferable.

Sharnay's system is generated from one construction. The aperture geometry produces the mark; the mark's angles produce the icon grid; the icon grid's proportions inform the layout grid; the layout grid's rhythm sets the motion timing. Nothing is chosen in isolation, and nothing needs to be defended in isolation — each element is correct because of what it descends from.

### Drawn from the instrument

Every form in this system has an antecedent in photographic equipment or process: aperture blades, frame lines, focal planes, contact sheets, sprocket holes, registration marks, exposure scales, f-stop progressions.

This is not nostalgia and it is not skeuomorphism. These forms are being used because they are already precise, already meaningful to the audience, and already free of decoration. A frame line is the most honest graphic device available to a photographer's brand: it does exactly one thing, and it is the same thing the brand does.

### Silent by default

The system's resting state is near-invisible. Black, white, paper, space, and a single line. Emphasis is available — a colour, a heavier weight, a larger mark — but it is spent rarely and deliberately, the way a photographer spends a highlight.

If the identity is noticeable in a piece where the photography is strong, the identity is wrong.

## 03 · The Aperture System

**The generative core. Everything else in this document descends from it.**

An aperture is a set of overlapping blades rotating around a common centre, opening and closing through a defined progression. It is a mechanism, a symbol, and a geometry at the same time, which is rare and useful.

### What it must accomplish

- **Produce the mark.** The primary identity emerges from the construction rather than being drawn and then explained.
- **Define an angle set.** A blade count fixes the rotational increment, and that increment becomes the only set of angles permitted anywhere in the system — in icons, in diagonal rules, in crop marks.
- **Provide a scale.** An aperture opens and closes through discrete stops. That progression gives us a native scale for density, spacing, and emphasis, and it means the brand's proportional system is inherited from photography rather than borrowed from a design blog.
- **Behave as a state.** Open and closed are meaningful positions, not just poses. Open can signal expansion, arrival, and reveal; closed can signal focus, conclusion, and precision. This gives the mark something to do in motion without animating it decoratively.

### Constraints

- Legible as a solid form at 12mm and as a single stroke at 512px.
- Constructed on a circle, with every blade identical and every angle derived, so the geometry can be documented and rebuilt exactly.
- No gradients, no dimensionality, no lens flare. The aperture is a diagram, not an illustration.
- The construction drawing is itself a deliverable. Anyone should be able to rebuild the mark from first principles.

### Open decisions — Phase 2

Blade count and its consequences for the angle set. Whether the aperture appears as an outline, a solid, or a negative space. Whether a wordmark and a monogram both derive from it or only one. How far the geometry may be abstracted before it stops reading as an aperture — and whether it needs to read as one at all.

## 04 · Icon Family

**One family. One grid. One stroke. No exceptions.**

Icons are where identity systems fail most visibly, because icons are added over time by different people under time pressure. The defence is a construction so specific that drawing an off-system icon takes more effort than drawing a correct one.

### What it must accomplish

- Cover the practice honestly: capture, light, lens, exposure, retouch, delivery, print, session types, booking, contact, location, and the service categories that emerge later.
- Read at interface size and at print size without redrawing.
- Sit beside the mark without competing with it — the mark is the only form allowed to be the most refined thing on a page.
- Extend predictably, so an icon added in year two is indistinguishable in provenance from one drawn in month two.

### Constraints

- A single grid with a defined keyline area, live area, and padding.
- One stroke weight, with a documented optical exception rule for the rare case where it fails.
- Terminals, joins, and corner radii specified once and applied everywhere.
- Only angles from the aperture's rotational set. Arcs share a common radius family.
- Geometric construction, no freehand. Every icon must be reconstructible from circles, lines, and the angle set.

### Open decisions — Phase 2

Grid size and stroke weight, and whether a second optical size is needed for small interface use. Whether the family is purely linear or admits a filled variant for emphasis. The scope of the first set — the working assumption is a tight core of essentials rather than a broad library nobody uses.

## 05 · Editorial Elements

**The typographic furniture that makes a page feel published rather than posted.**

These are the small marks that signal editorial care: hairline rules, frame numbers, index numerals, captions, credit lines, registration marks, contact-sheet notation, folios. Individually they are almost nothing. Collectively they are the difference between a website and a publication.

### What it must accomplish

- Give every image a place to be captioned, credited, and numbered without the layout looking cluttered.
- Provide hierarchy without heavier type — a rule and a small caps label can do work that a bold heading would do crudely.
- Carry the sense that the work is catalogued. A photograph with a frame number implies a body of work, an archive, and a practice.
- Behave identically in print and on screen, so a spread and a page are recognizably siblings.

### Constraints

- Rules have a defined weight family, tied to the icon stroke, not invented per layout.
- Numerals are tabular wherever they are used as data: frame numbers, dates, f-stops, dimensions, prices.
- Captions are set in one size and one style. Variety here reads as indecision.
- Every element must have a function. Decorative flourishes are outside this system by definition.

### Open decisions — Phase 2

Whether numbering is sequential per series or continuous across the archive. The notation format for frame identifiers. Whether registration and crop marks appear as a live device on real layouts or only in the guidelines as an explanation of the thinking.

## 06 · Layout Language

**Grids built from photographic proportions rather than from web convention.**

A photographer's work already arrives in a defined set of shapes: 3:2, 4:5, 1:1, 16:9, 5:7 in print. A grid that fights those shapes produces awkward crops and inconsistent white space forever. A grid derived from them produces layouts where images sit as though the page were made for them, because it was.

### What it must accomplish

- Hold every native aspect ratio without cropping the work to fit a column.
- Define three distinct layout registers and when each is used: **full bleed** for immersion, **framed** for consideration, **contact sheet** for volume and range.
- Make negative space measurable. Margins are specified, not eyeballed, and the space around an image is treated as part of the composition.
- Translate across media, so a print spread and a screen layout share proportion even when they share nothing else.

### Constraints

- Margins are generous and consistent. When space is tight, content is reduced before margins are.
- Type aligns to the same grid as imagery. No floating elements.
- A maximum measure for body text, respected on every screen size.
- The grid is documented as a drawing, not a set of numbers in a file.

### Open decisions — Phase 2

Column count and its behaviour at each breakpoint. Baseline grid or spacing scale. Whether the layout system uses one grid with modes or genuinely separate grids per register. Which aspect ratios are officially supported and which are simply tolerated.

## 07 · Motion

**Optical, not digital.**

Motion in this system is derived from what happens inside a camera and in a darkroom, not from interface convention. The reference library is physical: the pull of a focus ring, the fade of an exposure, the sweep of a shutter, the rotation of aperture blades, the way an image rises in a developing tray.

### What it must accomplish

- Make transitions feel like optics rather than like software. A change of state should feel observed, not triggered.
- Direct attention the way a photographer does — by resolving one thing while the rest stays soft.
- Establish timing with a physical basis. Shutter speeds give a defensible set of durations, and using them consistently makes the whole system feel calibrated.
- Stay subordinate. Motion clarifies sequence and hierarchy; it never performs.

### The behaviours to develop

**Focus** — resolution rather than movement. Emergence from unresolved to sharp.
**Exposure** — appearance through light, not opacity alone.
**Shutter** — reveals with a defined edge and direction.
**Aperture** — the mark and its derived forms opening and closing as state.
**Depth** — layered movement at different rates, read as depth of field rather than as parallax novelty.

### Constraints

- One easing family across the system. Mixed curves read as multiple authorship.
- Durations drawn from a fixed scale. No arbitrary values.
- Nothing loops. Nothing bounces. Nothing draws attention to its own cleverness.
- Every behaviour has a reduced-motion equivalent that is still correct, not merely disabled.

### Open decisions — Phase 2

The duration scale and its mapping to shutter values. Whether focus transitions are genuinely worth their rendering cost on real image sizes. How much motion survives on a phone, which is where most of this will actually be seen.

## 08 · Instagram

**The feed as a contact sheet.**

Instagram is where the brand is seen most often and controlled least. A grid of nine is not a set of nine posts; it is one composition that changes every time something is added, which means it has to be planned like a spread.

### What it must accomplish

- Compose the grid deliberately, in rows of three, with rhythm between full-frame images, framed images, and typographic panels.
- Give a single image room to be looked at, rather than surrounding it with graphics.
- Make series legible through carousels, so a body of work reads as a body of work.
- Provide templates that take minutes and hold quality when made quickly and on a phone.
- Establish caption conventions — how work is titled, credited, located, and dated — so the writing is as consistent as the design.

### Constraints

- Templates are few and strict. A large template library becomes a soft library within a month.
- Type on image is rare, and when it appears it obeys the same scale as everywhere else.
- No filters, no borders that are not part of the system, no per-post improvisation.
- Every template must survive being cropped by the platform.

### Open decisions — Phase 5

The rhythm pattern for the grid. Whether typographic panels earn their place or interrupt the work. Whether stories share the feed's language or have a looser, faster register of their own.

## 09 · Print

**Where the promise is confirmed.**

Print is the most persuasive surface available and the least forgiving. A card is held, turned over, and judged in under two seconds, and paper weight is a stronger argument than anything written on it.

### What it must accomplish

- Give the identity physical presence — weight, texture, edge, and the specific pleasure of a well-made small object.
- Make delivery part of the work. The moment a client receives prints should feel authored, not shipped.
- Prove the system survives production constraints, which is the real test of any mark.
- Establish a small, coherent family of pieces rather than a catalogue of collateral.

### Constraints

- Every piece must be producible at a sane cost. A system that requires an unreasonable print budget is a system that gets abandoned.
- Finishes are chosen for one reason at a time. Foil, emboss, and duplex do not appear together on the same piece.
- Specifications are written for a printer, not for a mood board: stock, weight, colour, finish, tolerance.
- Uncoated stock is the working assumption unless a specific piece argues otherwise.

### Open decisions — Phase 6

Which pieces make the core set. Whether the mark is best expressed as an emboss, a foil, or simply printed well. The paper family, which should be decided by holding samples rather than by reading specifications.

---

## 10 · Digital

**Everything on a screen, and the coherence between all of it.**

The website is the largest digital surface but not the only one. Gallery delivery, email, invoices, contracts, proposals, and PDF guides are all places where the brand is encountered — often at the moment a client is deciding whether to pay, which makes them worth more attention than they usually get.

### What it must accomplish

- Make the site a gallery: images set the pace, the interface recedes, and navigation never competes with a photograph.
- Extend the same language to the transactional surfaces, so an invoice looks like it came from the same studio as the portfolio.
- Hold up under real conditions — slow connections, small screens, bright daylight, and the browser's own defaults.
- Meet accessibility as a design requirement rather than a remediation step: contrast, focus states, motion preferences, and alternative text treated as part of the composition.

### Constraints

- Images are the only thing permitted to be visually loud.
- Component behaviour is inherited from the design system. Nothing is invented at build time.
- Performance targets are set before design begins, because they are a design constraint, not an engineering afterthought.
- Every interface state — empty, loading, error, focused — is designed. Undesigned states are where identities leak.

### Open decisions — Phase 4

The site's information architecture and how series are structured. How galleries are paced, and whether the viewer or the design controls that pace. Whether the journal is part of the launch or a later addition.
