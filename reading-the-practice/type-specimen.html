The Hand

Choosing a cursive for the letter.

The brief is specific: the Palmer Method, as taught in Venezuelan schools. That is a real constraint and a useful one, because it rules out almost every script font a search would surface. Palmer is not wedding calligraphy. It was a business hand, built for speed, and the things that make it recognisable are the things most decorative scripts get wrong.

Monoline
One even stroke weight throughout. No thick and thin. This alone disqualifies Copperplate and Spencerian faces, which swell on the downstroke.
Arm movement
Written from the shoulder, not the fingers, which produces an even unhurried rhythm rather than the bounce of a marker script.
Plain capitals
Palmer stripped away the flourishes Spencer loved. The capitals are ovals, not fireworks.
Full loops
A modest x height with generous ascenders and descenders, looped so a whole word can be written without lifting the pen.
Steady slant
A consistent lean of roughly fifty degrees. Consistency matters more than the angle.

That points somewhere unexpectedly precise. Playwrite is a family by TypeTogether built for exactly this purpose: one cursive per country, drawn from the handwriting model actually taught in that country's primary schools. There is no Venezuela, but there is Colombia, and Colombian letra pegada descends from the same Palmer cartillas you would have been copying. Argentina, Mexico, Cuba and Peru are its neighbours in the same family, each with small differences worth looking at.

All nine faces below are self hosted, so this page works offline, and all nine are OFL licensed, so any of them can ship in a client deliverable. All nine were checked for the full Spanish set before being included here, accents, enye and inverted punctuation, so none of them will fall back mid word.

Playwrite CO · Colombia

Closest to the brief

Colombian letra pegada, taken straight from the school cartillas that descend from Palmer. Monoline, evenly slanted, looped descenders on the f and the q, and capitals that stay plain except for a little decoration on G, L and Z. This is the nearest thing to a Venezuelan school hand that exists as a typeface.

Not Palmer

Included so the comparison is honest. Monoline and connected, so it passes the first test, but the capitals flourish and the slant is steeper than a school hand. Elegant, and reads as a designer's script rather than as your handwriting.

There is a third option worth naming, because it is the only one that is genuinely your handwriting rather than an emulation. Write the salutation and your signature on paper, once, with a fountain pen. Scan them. We place them as artwork. A font is a very good stand in for a hand, but it is the same nine strokes every time, and yours would not be.
