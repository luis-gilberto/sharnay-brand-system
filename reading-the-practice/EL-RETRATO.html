Preludio

One-sentence definition

El Retrato is the custody system inside Reading the Practice: it holds eligible words the participant wrote, then returns them unchanged at defined stages — without improving, summarizing, interpreting, or exhibiting them as campaign copy.

01
Filosofía

Core philosophy

Statement Meaning in product
We hold eligible words exactly as she wrote themExact text only; mechanical trim at most. Eligibility is mechanical, never editorial.
We do not improve itNo paraphrase, ranking by "meaning," or editorial rewrite
Archive is return, not storageThe emotional event is your words came back unchanged
The words are the only artifactInterface, institution, and labels nearly disappear
Recognition, not displayShe should feel received — not processed or presented
On eligibility

Eligibility is decided by mechanism, not by judgement. A fragment is held when it comes from a textarea, is non-empty after trim, and falls within portraitMaxChars. Nothing is ever assessed for quality, relevance, insight, or meaning.

A fragment that fails eligibility is not held and is never shown. A fragment that passes is returned unchanged at every stage that shows it. The system exercises judgement about fit, never about worth.

Rejected metaphors for Archive

Mirror-as-cleverness, testimonial quote, museum-wall poster, card UI, "ARCHIVO" as hero title.

Locked Archive metaphor

Return Plate — recto leaf in a dark room.

Sequence of feeling:

1The words remain.
2The words return.

Escenografía

Stages of The Portrait

Three custody stages, inserted after specific sections.

Stage After section Fragments shown Emotional job
Archivework1 — most recent eligibleReturn — one leaf, words only
Collectionvoice2–3Held together — still her words, not a collage of branding
Exhibitionexists1 — deterministic, lowest question № in sectionQuiet caption energy — still custody, not campaign
Why the three selection rules differ

The three stages select deterministically, but not identically. The rules differ because the jobs differ. None of them evaluates meaning, ranks quality, or reads content.

  • Archive takes the most recent eligible fragment, because its job is immediate return. The words she just finished writing come straight back to her.
  • Collection preserves ascending writing order, because its job is accumulation and relationship. The sequence is the record of how the thinking arrived, and reordering it would be an editorial act.
  • Exhibition takes the lowest questionN in its section, because it needs exactly one fragment chosen by a rule that cannot be mistaken for a judgement about which sentence is best.

Do not homogenise these into a single rule. The differences are load-bearing, and each one is the shape of that stage's emotional job.

Eligibility, all stages:

  • Portrait enabled (ENGAGEMENT.portraitEnabled)
  • Field type is textarea
  • Non-empty after trim
  • Length ≤ portraitMaxChars (180)
Hard content rule

Empty · too long · not textarea → never held. If a stage has fewer than minFragments, the portrait screen is skipped.

Skip / select logic lives in isPortraitEligible, upsertPortraitFragment, selectPortraitFragments, shouldSkipPortrait.

One name per chapter · locked

A chapter has one name, used identically in the black threshold and over the photograph: same words, same line break, same side. There is no separate short label for the threshold.

This is what makes the reveal read as one moment rather than two screens. If the two ever diverge, the title appears to jump when the photograph arrives, and the transition becomes a cut. The line break lives in titleLines and is written by hand, because a long title does not fit the chapter column.

4.2 Two elements

  1. Place — chapter name; where you are in the reading
  2. Time remaining — approximate minutes left (~ N min restantes · English equivalent)

Time recalculates at chapter start from typing pace (useTimeRemaining), floored to whole minutes, capped by the room seed, never shows seconds. Below one minute → singular "1 min." At zero → nothing.

4.3 RoomThreshold

Between rooms: full-viewport black field #0D0D0D. The threshold is not a screen of its own — it is the moment before the chapter appears, so it borrows the chapter’s layout exactly:

  • Prefix: "Entrando a" (mono, gold #C8A96A)
  • Chapter name: display serif, roman, never italic (VLS reserves italic for quotation and editorial voice)
  • Same side as the chapter: data-side is inherited from the frame, so the title lands where the photograph will put it
  • Same line break: titleLines, so a two-line title is two lines in both places
  • Time: mono, muted cream, absolutely positioned so it never pushes the title

Measured tolerance: horizontal offset between threshold title and chapter title is 0px at 1440, 970 and 390. Vertical residue averages 10–20px and comes from leading, not from naming.

Timing: 400 ms fade in → 800 ms hold → 400 ms fade out (1600 ms total).

prefers-reduced-motion: reduce → 300 ms crossfade of place/time only, no black field. aria-live announces entry + minutes.

The questions · editorial register

Twenty-five questions. Every one asks about something that happened, not about an intention: a session, a recommendation, what a client took away, what she prints, what she takes care of when nobody notices.

This is deliberate. A memory cannot be answered with borrowed vocabulary, and a portrait built from remembered detail is worth more than one built from self-description. The register is a conversation before a portrait — never branding, consulting, workshop, positioning or strategy language.

4.4 The pause · editorial voice

One reflection, one screen

Six times in the reading, Luis answers back: “Ahora puedo escuchar el trabajo, no solo verlo.” These lines used to share a screen with the announcement of the next chapter, which made them read as a caption to a heading.

The reflection now takes its own screen: one sentence, one photograph, nothing else. No chapter label, no dek, no credit. Anything else on the screen turns the pause back into a preamble.

ElementRule
Screen kindpause · renderPause()
PlacementOpposite the subject. data-side comes from the frame, never fixed — a fixed side puts the sentence over a face.
First reflectionNone. Before the first chapter there is nothing to reflect on yet.
AdvanceTwo beats. One gesture retires the sentence, the next continues. Never automatic.
Entry1400 ms. A gesture during entry settles the line rather than being spent.
The sequence

questions → pause (reflection alone) → threshold (black, title in place) → chapter (photograph arrives, title does not move)

The whole point is the join between the last two. The title must not move, because what stays fixed is what reads as one moment; what moves reads as two screens.

Chrome behavior on Archive

On Archive Return Plate, chrome shows brand + time only — no "Archivo" / stage title. The leaf carries the revelation.

The VLS governs appearance: the mark, the palette, the four voices, photographic devices and scrim. This specification governs behaviour: sequence, states, orientation, and the custody of what a person writes.

A rule about appearance belongs upstream even when it was discovered while building this product. A rule about behaviour belongs here even when it has a visible result. If a change to this document would alter how the brand looks anywhere else, it is in the wrong file.

5.4 One screen, one view · locked

The document does not scroll

A screen is a view, not a page. At every size, on every device, the whole of a screen is visible at once. There is one exception: the answer review, where moving through what she wrote is the task itself. It scrolls inside its own box, so the fixed bars never move.

ElementRule
html, body, #appoverflow: hidden · height fixed to the viewport
Bar allowanceMeasured at runtime into --bar-top / --bar-bottom. The bars use min-height, so their real height can exceed --chrome-h; reserving the token alone leaves the panel under the bottom bar.
Every screenFits the band between the two bars
Review onlyoverflow-y: auto · overscroll-behavior: contain
How a screen is made to fit

CSS cannot know whether a text fits: it has to be measured. fitScreen() writes a single number, --fit, and every scalable value on the screen is multiplied by it. When everything fits, --fit is 1 and nothing is reduced. Only when it does not fit does the screen yield, by bisection, until it does.

Measure the content, not the box. The panel carries height: 100%, so its box does not grow with what is inside it; measuring the box reports the same height at every scale and the search never converges. What is measured is the extent of the children against the visible band.

Nothing is ever cut. If a screen still does not fit at the floor, it scrolls inside its own box rather than hiding a word. Hiding what she has to read is not an available outcome, and neither is hiding what we asked her.

Open question · the floor

The floor is currently 0.48, which is too low to be a standard: at that scale the microcopy is around seven pixels. On a phone, the letter, the heavy questions and the send screen still reach it, which means those screens hold more than a phone can show.

That is an editorial problem, not a typographic one. The options are a legibility floor with those screens split in two, moving the “why I ask” panel out of the flow so it no longer competes for the same space, or accepting that some blocks are optional and are dropped rather than shrunk. Not yet decided.

06
Film · apertura de capítulo

Chapter openers · editorial voice + dek

A chapter opener is a full-bleed film screen where the system briefly reappears as editorial voice, acknowledges progress, introduces a new narrative territory, and hands the participant into the next dimension of the intake. It does more than separate — it opens.

Naming · canonical

English: chapter opener. Spanish: apertura de capítulo. Retired: chapter separator · separador de capítulo.

6.1 Two text layers · not two subheadings

A chapter separator contains two distinct textual layers that must never share typographic treatment:

Layer Role Voice
Editorial intervention Recognizes progress, interprets what is happening, hands the participant forward. The system speaking editorially.
Dek Explains concretely what the chapter will explore. Functional description of scope.
Canonical typographic rule

The editorial intervention uses serif italic. Italic is reserved for the editorial voice.

The dek of a chapter separator must use serif roman. Dek and functional descriptive copy are never italic.

Never use serif italic simultaneously for both. The dek must read functional, stable, descriptive.

6.2 Anatomy of the separator

The right-half editorial column, top to bottom:

Element Family / style Size (desktop) Notes
Editorial intervention Cormorant · italic 400 28–36px · lh 1.3 ~76% cream · max 430px · max 3 lines · top of the right panel · separated vertically from the chapter block below
Eyebrow Mono 500 uppercase 10px · tracking 0.28em Text: Capítulo · color: ámbar
Title Cormorant · roman 300 clamp(3rem, 6.4vw, 5.2rem) Sentence case · may break to 2 lines · .title .row spans as nowrap
Dek Cormorant · roman 400 · font-style normal 18–22px · lh 1.5 Max ~390px · contrast equal to or slightly higher than the intervention · directly below the title · never italic
Technical caption Mono 500 uppercase · with serif name 11px · tracking 0.22em Below the dek · same left edge · single line on desktop
Left edge · non-negotiable

The editorial intervention, eyebrow, title, dek, and technical caption all share the same left edge. Do not create a micro-column to the right of the dek for the caption.

6.3 Technical caption · two canonical variants

The photographic classification is approved editorial content, never placeholder. The layout adapts to the classification — not the other way around. Do not simplify. Do not drop location qualifiers. Do not substitute.

Two canonical rendering variants, chosen by the length of the classification:

Variant A · short classification · one line

Editorial portrait · Studio | By Erika Sharnay

A single line with the | divider between classification and byline. Used when the classification fits comfortably in the editorial column at desktop.

Variant B · long classification · two lines

Environmental fashion portrait · Night location
By Erika Sharnay

Two intentional lines. No | divider. Classification on line 1 · byline on line 2. Vertical gap ~5–7px. Both lines share the same left edge.

Unbreakable units — in both variants:

  • The full classification (e.g. ENVIRONMENTAL FASHION PORTRAIT · NIGHT LOCATION) — never breaks internally
  • By Erika Sharnay — never breaks between "By" and the name

Never:

  • A dangling | at the start of line 2 (orphan divider).
  • A break inside Environmental fashion portrait or inside Night location.
  • Splitting By from Erika Sharnay.
  • Shrinking type aggressively to force variant A when variant B is the honest fit.

Which variant to use: if the classification comfortably fits one line at the editorial column width without dropping below the minimum type size, use A. Otherwise, use B intentionally — as a compositional decision, not a wrap accident.

Right edge · non-negotiable

Do not create multiple left edges or right edges without an explicit compositional intention.

6.5 Chrome + navigation

Standard chrome-top applies. Chapter opener specific behavior:

  • Place-info shows the next chapter name + minutes remaining (e.g. Cierre | ~ 2 min restantes)
  • Progress rail: full low-opacity rail + active fill in ámbar · position: immediately below chrome top row · aligned to global gutters
  • Continuar remains outline — it is a continuation action inside the flow, not a primary entry
CTA behavior on chapter openers

Unlike the intake's other film surfaces where the CTA is cream-fill (specimen v1), a chapter opener uses outline. The distinction: cream-fill announces a primary entry (start / open); outline announces continuity within a journey already underway.

6.6 Photographic treatment

Preserve the image and its full composition — signage, crowd, night context, and the relationship between subject and location. Do not crop out narrative content in service of tidy type placement.

Dark treatment must preserve
  • Reds and magentas at their real saturation
  • Skin tone integrity
  • Fabric texture in the dress
  • Ambient depth (foreground vs. midground vs. background)
  • Subject-to-background separation

Do not apply a uniform gray veil. Do not cool the image globally. Do not desaturate.

Localized scrims · the only permitted intervention

Two low-opacity radial pools, anchored where legibility requires it:

  1. Right-column scrim — protects the editorial column from competing highlights.
  2. Bottom-left scrim — protects the nav-utility from bright floor / dress reflections.

Both scrims must feel invisible over darker photographic areas — they only appear where highlights would otherwise fight the type. Never a global overlay.

6.7 Chrome brand mark

The Sharnay Photography logomark is the anchor of the chrome. Not a lockup: a lockup is the mark with the words, and the chrome never carries one. Reference: the approved "El retrato" cover.

  • Editorial breathing scale on desktop: height: 56px · max-width: 360px · opacity: 0.96
  • Never compressed — the logomark must keep its clear space; do not rebuild a lockup from mark plus separately set words
  • Kept baseline-aligned with the place-info on the right of the chrome row
  • Filter is not applied on film mode — the PNG ships cream and stays cream
  • On paper the same asset carries filter: brightness(0). That is a tone correction for a light ground, not a recolour of the brand, and it is the only filter the mark ever takes
  • The size ladder is height driven first and width driven second, so a short window and a narrow window cannot contradict each other. --chrome-h steps with it: 92 / 86 / 80 / 82 / 74
Responsive scale
  • Desktop: 56px height
  • ≤ 800h · 50px
  • ≤ 680h · 44px
  • Tablet: 46px
  • Mobile: 40px

6.8 Casing rules

  • Chapter titles → sentence case in Spanish. "Lo que ya existe" · not "Lo Que Ya Existe."
  • Chapter titles → "Color y sensación" · not "Color y Sensación."
  • Eyebrow Capítulo → written as a proper noun in the copy; rendered UPPERCASE via CSS only.
  • Technical caption → UPPERCASE via CSS; the participant-facing name By Erika Sharnay stays in serif sentence case (unbreakable unit).

6.9 Rhythm

Film ↔ paper alternation remains the structural rhythm of the intake: work fills the room, then leaves so she can write. A chapter opener is the moment before the room empties.

6.10 Explicit typographic rules · locked

The ten rules of the chapter opener
  1. The editorial intervention is the voice of the system. Not a subtitle. Not decoration.
  2. The editorial intervention uses serif italic (locked). Italic is reserved for editorial intervention.
  3. The dek of a chapter opener must use serif roman. Dek and functional descriptive copy are never italic.
  4. Never use italic simultaneously for editorial intervention and dek within the same screen.
  5. The dek is descriptive and functional — it explains what the chapter will explore.
  6. The chapter title in Spanish uses sentence case.
  7. The technical caption shares the same left edge as eyebrow, title, and dek. No micro-columns.
  8. The photographic classification is approved editorial content, never placeholder.
  9. The layout adapts to the classification — not the other way around.
  10. The caption has two canonical variants: A (one line, | divider) for short classifications · B (two lines, no |) for long classifications.

6.11 Portrait composition · face clearance

Text must never cover a face

On any photographic portrait screen (pause, chapter opener, or other film stage), editorial copy must never cover or visually cross a subject’s face. Protected area: forehead, eyes, nose, mouth, jawline, and the primary hair silhouette immediately surrounding the face.

Placement must respond to the image’s actual negative space (garment, torso, empty field, opposite half), not only to a generic grid or optical center. Use per-frame copyBand / side metadata when a photograph requires a different mobile band than the default.

The pause screen (single editorial intervention over film) follows the same italic voice rule as the chapter-opener intervention. It has no dek — so italic is unambiguous.

Collection · emergence, not return

Collection is the first moment a portrait begins to emerge — through proximity alone, from 2–3 exact participant fragments, with no explanation of why they belong together.

Constellation v1 · three-fragment template

Same field. Different positions. Equal dignity.

Three fragments land in one continuous warm field. Slot 1 at top-left, slot 2 mid-center-right, slot 3 lower-left. Vertical rhythm ≈ 24% / 19% of field height between slot tops — irregular on purpose.

No cards. No rules. No labels. The relationship is spatial. Empty air upper-right and lower-right is intentional.

9.3 What Collection is · what Collection is not

Is:

  • 2–3 held textarea answers from the Voice section (sectionId: 'voice'), exact text after trim
  • One field — the "room" — where fragments coexist
  • Emergence through spatial relationship
  • Continuous with the writing voice of the intake (Cormorant Garamond), not Archive's EB Garamond Return Plate
Is not
  • A second Return Plate
  • A dark room + pale leaf ceremony
  • An exhibition caption
  • A labeled "COLLECTION" moment
  • A feed, list, stacked cards, or carousel — especially on mobile

9.4 Content rules · non-negotiable

  1. Exact participant words only — mechanical trim; no paraphrase.
  2. No interpretation, summary, categorization, commentary, AI insight.
  3. No hierarchy of importance — no featured fragment, no size-as-meaning, no "hero" quote.
  4. No cards, labels, numbering, quote marks as decoration.
  5. No masonry / Pinterest / dashboard energy.
  6. Eligibility: textarea only · non-empty after trim · length ≤ 180.
  7. Stage gate: minFragments: 2, maxFragments: 3. If fewer than 2 eligible fragments from Voice → skip Collection entirely.
  8. Selection order: deterministic by questionN ascending — writing order. Never rank by "meaning."
  9. Integrity: never truncate, hyphenate for fit, or crop. If a fragment cannot fit at the minimum viable size, do not mangle it.

The type-hierarchy rule

No hierarchy of meaning via type. Size differences exist only to keep long lines from dominating the field optically — never to feature a "best" answer.

9.8 Length handling

Upstream already caps at 180 characters. Within that:

Length (chars) Behavior in field
≤ 48Short measure (~22–24ch) · may sit in slot 1 or 3 comfortably
49 – 120Mid/long measure (~28–30ch) · prefer mid or right-leaning slots
121 – 180Long measure · slightly smaller optical size (~92–96% of base) · never truncate

Slot assignment: by writing order (questionN), not by length. Length only affects measure and optical size within the assigned slot.

Overflow protocol

If after placement a fragment overflows the field bounds:

  1. Shrink that fragment's font in 1px steps to a floor of 17px
  2. If still overflowing, reduce max-width by 2ch steps to a floor of 20ch

Terminal branch. If the fragment still exceeds the field bounds at both floors, remove that fragment from the current Collection composition and resolve the stage again using the remaining eligible fragments. If fewer than two eligible fragments remain, skip Collection.

Never clip, truncate, ellipsize, hyphenate, or partially render participant text. Dropping a fragment whole is legal. Showing part of one is not.

9.9 Mobile · compressed constellation

The problem

A naive stack becomes a feed / vertical list / cards. That destroys emergence.

The solution: on viewports ≤ 720px (or when the field is portrait-narrow):

  1. Keep one continuous field filling the stage · same warm field, no cards.
  2. Keep distinct horizontal offsets — fragments must not share the same left edge.
  3. Keep irregular vertical rhythm — unequal gaps (e.g. 1.35em / 2.1em / 1.6em via % tops).
  4. Do not full-width block each fragment. Max-width ~78–86% of field, left offsets vary (8% / 18% / 10%).
  5. Do not use separators, numbers, or swipe carousels.
  6. Prefer one viewport: scale type down (clamp(1.05rem, 4.2vw, 1.22rem)) · compress slot tops (14% / 36% / 58%).
  7. If content still exceeds the chrome gap: allow soft scroll of the field only, no snap points, no item chrome — must still read as one room, not a feed.

15.3 What Exhibition is · is not

Is:

  • 1 held textarea answer from section exists (deterministic: lowest questionN)
  • Exact text after mechanical trim
  • A quiet field where attention can remain
  • Continuous with intake voice: Cormorant Garamond 400
  • Feeling: I stayed with this
Is not
  • A room that must be "designed" as architecture
  • A museum, gallery, caption, plaque, or wall
  • A second Return Plate — no leaf, no EB Garamond, no dark ceremony
  • A Collection of one — no constellation of a singleton
  • Quote graphics, pull-quotes, or editorial hierarchy inside the sentence

15.4 Content rules · non-negotiable

  1. Exact participant words only.
  2. No interpretation · summary · categorization · commentary · system voice.
  3. No labels, titles, metadata, or institutional language on the field.
  4. No quote marks added by the system.
  5. No emphasis, bold, italic, color shift, or size change within the sentence.
  6. No visual devices that explain regard — rules under text, brackets, sightline strokes, crosshairs, frames.
  7. Eligibility: textarea, non-empty trim, ≤ portraitMaxChars (180).
  8. Stage gate: minFragments: 1, maxFragments: 1. If none eligible → skip.
  9. Selection: lowest questionN in sectionId: 'exists'. Never rank by "meaning."
  10. Never truncate, hyphenate, ellipsis, or crop text.

The question v1 answered

16.2 Not a chapter opener

Do not inherit chapter opener rules

The chapter opener architecture (editorial intervention + eyebrow + title + dek, two-voice layering) applies only to chapter openers. The closing letter has one main voice: Luis. The system voice retreats — Luis takes the word personally.

Do not add a system editorial intervention above the letter. Doing so introduces a third voice (system + Luis + UI) that flattens Luis's ownership of the deliverable.
